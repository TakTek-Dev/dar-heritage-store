Everything here
starts with a stitch

The DAR logo is embroidered on a grid of 44 stitches. This system takes that same grid and builds the whole site with it: spacing, corners, icons and motion. So when the logo is absent, the stitch still speaks the name of the house.

Start with the stitch
01

Three principles

Every decision in the interface goes back to one of them. If an element serves none of them, we remove it.

The thobe is the page

The background is linen cloth, and the embroidery sits where it sits on a thobe (the traditional embroidered dress): the chest panel (qabbeh) at the top, the side panel (bnayqa) along the edge, and the hem (dhayyal) at the bottom.

The stitch is the unit

Every space is a multiple of 8 pixels, and every corner is stepped two stitches deep, like the petals of the star. There are no rounded corners in DAR.

The product is the hero

Ornament is a frame, not a painting. When a photo or video of a handmade piece appears, the embroidery steps back and gives it the space.

02

The stitch

One stitch equals 8 pixels. The star is 44 stitches wide, icons are 12 stitches, and bands repeat every 6 or 7 stitches. That is why every part looks as if it came off the same loom.

05

Motifs

A small library drawn as real embroidery charts, one character per stitch. The same chart comes out as clean squares at small sizes and as cross-stitches at large sizes. DAR reviews the names and meanings of the motifs before publishing.

The DAR star

From the logo. For big moments only: the opening, loading, and order confirmation.

Cypress

A vertical divider between sections, and a marker for categories.

Amulet

The mark of guarantee and authenticity, next to the words “Handmade”.

Starlet

The rhythm dot: before small headings, in lists, and between words.

08

Motion

Motion at DAR is the motion of the needle: stitch after stitch, from the centre outwards or along the line. No bounce and no shine. Anyone who asks for reduced motion sees everything ready at once.

09

Rules

What protects the signature as the site grows and new pages and a dashboard are added.

We do

  • Build every space, corner and icon on the 8-pixel stitch
  • Place embroidery where it sits on a thobe: top, side and bottom
  • Photograph products on linen, in daylight, with a hand or a stitch close by
  • Name the village or city the motif came from
  • Keep Henna for stitches, large headings, one buy button and one section

We don’t

  • Rounded corners, soft shadows, gradients, glass
  • Colours from outside the palette, even for offers and sales
  • Motifs drawn with curved lines; embroidery is squares only
  • Embroidery over a product photo or behind long text
  • Icons from ready-made libraries, or emoji
