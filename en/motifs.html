The Star (Najmeh)

The eight-pointed star, which many call the Star of Bethlehem

An eight-pointed star that appears again and again on dresses from many of Palestine's cities and villages. It is stitched at the heart of the chest piece and on the sleeves, and people read it as a guiding light. We drew the DAR logo from it, stitch by stitch.

Where you see it
The chest piece and the sleeves, and the DAR logo. The little starlet sits beside every heading on this site.

The Cypress (Saru)

The cypress tree

An evergreen that keeps its leaves through every winter. It is stitched in rows, one after another, down the sides and edges of the dress, and people read it as steadfastness and long life.

Where you see it
The sides and edges of the dress, in rows.
A row of amulets
03

The Amulet (Hijab)

The triangle, or the charm

A triangle stitched like a charm, which people say protects the wearer from the evil eye. Threads hang from its base like tassels, and inside it sits a smaller triangle in another colour.

Where you see it
The chest of the dress, alone or in a row.
Ears of wheat one after another, as in a field
04

Wheat Ears (Sanabel)

Ears of wheat

A sign of plenty and a good harvest, from the life of villages that lived off the land and what it gave. They are stitched one after another, like a field bending in the wind.

Where you see it
The hems and sleeves, in rows.
The chain as it runs on
05

The Chain (Silsileh)

Linked rings

Stitches linked like rings, which frame the motifs and divide them while tying them together. No large motif is complete without it.

Where you see it
Around the large motifs, and along the top of every page on the DAR site.
The edge as it closes the hem
06

Saw Teeth (Snan al-Munshar)

Edge triangles

Triangles in a row that close the edge of the cloth like a little wall, and finish the embroidery at the hem and the sleeves.

Where you see it
The hem and sleeves, and just above the footer of this site.

A dress's chest piece tells its city

The qabbeh is the embroidered panel on the chest, and it is the first thing you read on a dress. Every city has its own, and you know it by its colours and motifs before you ask.

FallahiCross-stitch

Two stitches crossing in an X over a single square of cloth. The best-known village stitch, and every motif on this page is made with it.

TahririCouching in silk and metal thread

A cord of silk or metal thread draws curving lines, held down on the cloth by small stitches. Bethlehem is famous for it; you can see it on its chest piece in the picture above.
